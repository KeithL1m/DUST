# ☕ Morning Coffee — September 7, 2026

## Iran War Spreads as Houthis Strike Saudi Aramco, Oil Tops $90

**A Houthi strike hit Saudi Aramco's Jazan refinery on September 7, adding a fifth country to a conflict that expanded dramatically after the US struck Iran on September 1 and Tehran retaliated against Jordan, Bahrain, Kuwait, and Iraq.**

The September 1 US strikes on Iranian air defense and military sites killed at least 12 people, including five at a wedding in Kuhestak. Iran retaliated September 2 with missiles into Jordan (13 fired, 10 intercepted) and drones targeting Bahrain's Sheikh Isa airbase and US facilities in Erbil, Iraq.

Supreme Leader Khamenei promised Washington "unforgettable lessons," and today's unclaimed Aramco strike, reported by the Financial Times, sent WTI crude up 1.27% past $90.

**Why it matters:** Jordan and Kuwait had no prior stake in this conflict, so each new country pulled in — and now Gulf oil infrastructure itself as a target — raises the odds of a wider regional war rather than a contained one.

## US Peace Push in Ukraine Ends Without Breakthrough as Strikes Continue

**US envoys Witkoff and Kushner spent three hours with Putin in Moscow on September 5, then met Zelensky in Kyiv on September 6 — both sides called it "substantive," but no deal emerged, and Russian drones hit a Zaporizhzhia mall overnight.**

Putin told the envoys he expects Russia to meet its "goals" and wants the war's "root causes" addressed, signaling no retreat from demands that Ukraine cede Donbas and drop NATO aspirations. Zelensky said afterward, "we expect the war will continue," and is counting on a winter aid package for air defense and grid protection.

Hours later, drones burned roughly 700 square meters of Zaporizhzhia's City Mall, though no deaths were reported since it had been closed.

**Why it matters:** Zelensky's own framing — bracing for a winter of continued war rather than a ceasefire — signals Kyiv isn't banking on this diplomatic round changing anything on the ground.

## Trump to Host State Dinner for Xi as China Courts "Stable Alternative" Image

**Trump announced he'll host a state dinner for Xi Jinping around September 24 in Washington, even as he publicly asked Xi not to support Iran — the same week Xi wrapped a diplomatic tour positioning China as a steadier partner than the US.**

Xi was received in Cairo on September 1 as part of what the Washington Post called a "rare flurry" of trips, while Trump confirmed the dinner plan and said he'd directly asked Xi to stay out of backing Iran as his administration pressures countries and firms linked to Tehran.

Domestically, Xi chaired a July 30 Politburo meeting setting an October Fifth Plenum as a runway to the 2027 Party Congress, with growth targeted at 4.5-5% against a 4.3% Q2 print.

**Why it matters:** Hosting Xi for a state dinner while simultaneously pressuring him over Iran shows Washington needs Beijing's cooperation even while the two compete for global influence elsewhere.

## Migrant Children Held Far Beyond Legal Limit at Texas Detention Center

**At least 60 children have been held over 100 days at the Dilley, Texas family detention center — five times the 20-day court-ordered limit — with Rep. Joaquin Castro calling it "in perpetual violation of the law."**

Roughly 3,000 children have passed through Dilley since its March 2025 reopening, more than half held past 20 days with average stays hitting 47 days in some stretches.

Castro has made seven oversight visits demanding closure, and immigrant-rights lawyers have renewed legal action alleging violations of the Flores settlement governing child detention limits.

**Why it matters:** This is a binding-court-settlement fight, not just a policy dispute — if the Flores litigation succeeds, it could force changes at every family detention facility, not just Dilley.

## Central Banks Split Heading Into a Pivotal September

**Markets are split roughly 60-40 on a Fed rate hike this month, the ECB decides September 10 after one more inflation reading, and Bank of Japan board member Hajime Takata is pushing for a hike as inflation nears target.**

The Fed held rates at 3.50%-3.75% in July on a vote where three policymakers wanted an immediate hike; the BOJ rejected a move to 1.25% that same month on an 8-1 vote, but Takata warned September 2 that "risks of overheating are rising."

Analysts currently expect the Bank of Canada and Bank of England to hold steady while the ECB, BOJ, and Reserve Bank of New Zealand lean toward raising.

**Why it matters:** A genuinely split picture across major central banks makes currency and bond markets more sensitive to each individual decision than during a period of synchronized policy — watch the ECB's September 10 call first, since it lands before the Fed's.

## Japan Scraps Spending Caps, Plans Record $895B Budget

**Japan's Takaichi administration eliminated budget caps on AI, semiconductor, and other strategic spending for fiscal 2027, part of a record ¥143 trillion ($895 billion) total budget request — the largest in the country's history.**

The government dropped its long-standing "fiscal consolidation" goal for what it calls the "first year of responsible proactive fiscal policy," following Takaichi's earlier abandonment of a firm annual budget target.

Bond-market reaction now functions as the main check on spending instead of self-imposed caps.

**Why it matters:** Removing spending caps while global bond yields are elevated is a real fiscal risk if investors start demanding a premium for Japanese debt — watch JGB yields in the coming weeks as the market's real-time verdict.

### 🔍 The Big Picture

The throughline today is expansion without resolution: the Iran war keeps drawing in new countries and new categories of targets, the Ukraine peace process keeps producing "substantive" talks that change nothing on the ground, and central banks can't agree among themselves on which direction to move. Even the diplomacy that is moving forward — the Trump-Xi state dinner — is happening alongside, not instead of, real friction over Iran.

What to watch this week: the ECB's September 10 decision as the first domino in this month's central-bank split, whether Saudi Arabia responds to the Aramco strike in a way that further widens the Middle East conflict, and whether the promised US-Russia-Ukraine trilateral talks actually materialize or join the list of peace pushes that produced talk but no deal.
